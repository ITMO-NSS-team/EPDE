Completed 2026-11-17: coefficient contrasts normalized by measurement noise

Do not replace dyad with the tested normalized contrast score. It removes
the coefficient-level denominator and passes the mathematical checks, but
performs much worse as a structural ranking objective. Uncertainty also
remains miscalibrated when generated around the reconstructed field.
These are separate failures, requiring separate responses.

Scope and frozen design

All 15 default systems, fresh anchor seeds 21/22/23, 5% Gaussian measurement
noise: 45 records and 60 equation/seed cases. Full panels contain 1050
candidate/anchor entries, including constants, registered equivalents and
9 equations without fitted features. Historical seed0 freezes geometry
and candidate fitting bank/method. Anchor data rebuilds other fit inputs.
This is a finite-panel conditional study, not a new evolutionary search.

The new score uses the same raw weak matrix, partitions, common slab floor
and minimum-norm slab solves as production dyad. It measures coefficient
differences between sibling slabs, with 256 full-field bootstrap draws
estimating each difference's noise bias and variance. Q_bias averages the
bias-corrected squared difference divided by (1+1/256)*variance, first
within partitions, then over levels and axes. Q_raw omits bias correction.
No coefficient-median denominator, covariance ridge or tuned mixing weight.
Joint field draws retain cross-window and cross-candidate dependencies;
the general score is not assumed chi-square.

Each anchor also has 128 independent bootstrap-check fields and 128 actual
benchmark noise fields. Main discrepancy is evaluated with fixed and
refitted coefficients; the latter reselects reconstruction smoothing.
Correction maps, geometry, weights and fitting-bank/method stay fixed.
Final runs used 11,520 calibration and 11,520 evaluation measurements.
Clean data is used only after method-input freezing, for diagnostics.

Why the primary replacement loses

Relative to dyad, Q_bias has 28 losses and no gains. Every loss has a
dominating candidate whose support strictly adds terms to the reference
with the same target. There are 55 such superset-dominator pairs, plus two
other dominators. Only two lost cases have any rank-deficient dominator;
this is not mainly a singular-solver failure.

Noise normalization asks whether a change is large relative to uncertainty.
It does not reward a precise, parsimonious representation. Averaging also
allows extra terms with small standardized changes to reduce the score.
In 46/55 superset pairs, median contrast variance for the shared reference
features increases; the median ratio across pairs is 1.094, with examples
above 20. Larger uncertainty can reduce the standardized evidence of drift.
These observations support the mechanism, rather than isolating a single
cause for every pair. Summing instead of averaging partially helps (44/60),
but its null expectation scales with feature count, adding a complexity
preference, and it still trails dyad.

Example: Lorenz21, equation0, reference -> add_10:
  features: 2 -> 3
  D:        0.00045109 -> 0.00037203
  Q_bias:   0.16233 -> 0.06524
  dyad:     0.004258 -> 0.256454
  shared-feature median contrast-variance ratio: 1.933
Q gives the larger model both better objectives, removing the reference.
Dyad retains a tradeoff. That is useful structural behavior, even though
dyad's near-zero coefficient denominator is unsuitable for straightforward
uncertainty interpretation. A test statistic is not automatically a good
model-selection objective.

Uncertainty calibration

The diagnostic uses independent-bootstrap 95th-percentile noise radii.
Benchmark errors are measured relative to clean coefficient contrasts,
whereas bootstrap errors are measured relative to pilot contrasts. This
tests the distribution of contrast noise, not whether a candidate is true.
No cutoff is used for search or pruning.

Mean coverage over 1041 candidate/anchor entries with contrasts: 73.58%.
Over registered entries: 53.48%; over the 60 references: 65.87%.
Mean per-coordinate coverage is 83.48%, and the median bootstrap/actual
coordinate SD ratio is 1.011. Reasonable variance scale does not imply
correct centering or correct multivariate coverage. These are different
diagnostics from the previous study's scalar-dyad intervals and must not be
compared as if they measured the same confidence statement.

Reference contrast-noise coverage is zero across all three anchors for
Burgers, KdV, forced KdV, KS, compound PDE and divide PDE. Other systems
have much better coverage, including Lorenz 94.36%, ODE 94.27%, LV 95.83%
and VDP 96.88%. Structural losses also occur on these better-calibrated
systems: reconstruction bias cannot explain the whole ranking failure.

Oracle attribution (diagnostic, unavailable in deployment)

Four core systems at anchor21 were rerun with known clean fields, first
keeping estimated noise and then using the known benchmark noise law.
Each control used 256 calibration and 128 check fields. To resolve the
short check run's sampling variation, a frozen follow-up added 1024 new
check and 1024 independent actual fields per system, for references only.
Both controls preserve the original 256 calibration draws. No oracle
choice feeds back into deployable scores or structural selection.

differences include finite Monte Carlo error; draws across candidates and
systems are not pooled as independent evidence.

For forced KdV and KS, changing the field center while retaining estimated
noise repairs the severe failure. This supports sensitivity of the noisy
coefficient-fit bias to reconstruction error. Simply estimating variance
more precisely or changing the dyad denominator does not address it.
Wave has a mixed result: a clean center alone does not improve coverage,
and the original 128-draw result had appreciable sampling variation.
The larger true-model control gives roughly 93-97% across the four cases.
It verifies the diagnostic's behavior under known inputs, not practical
calibration from noisy data.

Fixes and checks

The initial research extractor used a mathematically equivalent smaller QR
layout. Tiny arithmetic differences were amplified by near-zero medians
in production dyad replay. The extractor now reproduces production's full
QR column layout, including the unused intercept column, column indexing,
median arithmetic and axis reduction. Replay tolerance was not changed.
All initial attempts were archived in implementation_initial and all 45
cases rerun under the corrected sources; initial results are excluded.
This repairs the experimental implementation, not production dyad.

Checks cover zero and nonzero constant nulls, noise-scale invariance,
feature/target units, axis transposition, drift and explicit ODE_simple
energy/phase identities with constants. The known Gaussian toy gives
mean Q=0.98767 and nominal95% coverage=95.285% on 32768 independent draws.
819 real-panel replays have exactly zero dyad discrepancy. Explicit
constants and valid identities remain in scope; no constant ban was added.

Decision and next research question

Keep dyad as the current structural heuristic, with feature count as a
serious comparator. Do not promote Q, its summed variant, or the larger
three-objective front as a validated replacement. Preserve constants.
Separate support preference from uncertainty: a broad uncertainty band
means insufficient information, not stronger evidence for a larger model.

The next focused hypothesis is to calibrate raw weak residual moments for
fixed coefficient vectors before inverting them into coefficient confidence
sets. This may avoid bootstrapping a sensitive coefficient-fit map around
a biased reconstructed field. Existing Gaussian moment corrections can be
reused, but this extension is NOT implemented or validated here. It should
first pass independent raw-moment coverage checks on the failing systems;
any resulting coefficient sets may legitimately be broad or unbounded.
No new gate or penalty is justified by the current evidence.
