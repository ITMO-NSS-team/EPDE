Training-replica fitting: targeted mechanism test, 2026-11-16
Completed ac, wave, forced KdV, KS; three seeds each, 5% noise. Solver-free.

Symmetric cross-replica weak fitting has useful effects but is not a general
replacement. It improves wave fitting, while sacrificing KS accuracy and
leaving forced KdV biased. Mean correction helps AC but reverses some of
the wave gain. Do not pick a different estimator per system using clean data.

The old pre-adaptive wave fit still has smaller energy (0.001334) than the
best new replica variant (0.004658). Thus the wave improvement is relative
to the recent continuous adaptive fit, not the best historical wave result.

The 9/12 spectral outcome is not proof of better coefficients; the fixed
clean errors above and lower local retention expose this distinction.

Negative cross-Gram eigenvalues occur in some forced-KdV fits. They are
retained under the existing signed numerical solve; no PSD clipping or
threshold was chosen to force a preferred equation. Coefficients remain far
from [-6,-1,1] in these cases.

Clean training-replica controls have median frozen residual energies:
AC 2.21e-7; wave 4.57e-6; forced KdV 3.24e-6; KS 2.93e-5. The coarser time
sampling itself therefore contributes to the KS regression. Replica fitting
trades within-replica error correlation for less information per replica;
it cannot remove common reconstruction or support-selection bias.

All 12 cases pass frozen-score reproduction; scalar cross-Gram checks show
the expected removal of within-replica Gram noise bias under independent,
zero-mean linear operator errors. Real nonlinear reconstruction is not that
ideal model. Inputs/sources and finite-score coverage are audited separately.
No clean field was used to tune the method. No production changes were made.

This is a targeted four-system result, not a second core-15 benchmark.
The mixed mechanism result does not justify expanding it as a replacement.
