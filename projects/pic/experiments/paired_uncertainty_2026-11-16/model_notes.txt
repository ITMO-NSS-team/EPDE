Interpretation of the conditional uncertainty calculation

For a fixed candidate and training rotation g, let a and b be its two
held-out corrected weak residual vectors, and let e and f be perturbations
from independent raw groups. The exact score perturbation is

  [Re(<e,b> + <a,f> + <e,f>)] / training_target_energy.

The last term is essential when a physically correct residual is small.
A first-order delta-method approximation drops it and can miss uncertainty
near zero. The experiment evaluates all three terms and reports the
first-order and quadratic-only standard deviations separately.

For candidates i,j scored on the same observations,
  Var(S_i-S_j) = Var(S_i) + Var(S_j) - 2 Cov(S_i,S_j).
Independent candidate error bars omit the covariance. Likewise the three
rotations reuse groups: Var((S_0+S_1+S_2)/3) includes off-diagonal rotation
covariances. Group-level common indices/signs preserve both dependencies.

The operator-error pools have 128 reconstructed bootstrap draws per group.
The same sample index maps to the same reconstructed field in every test
bank. Each complete field draw carries all its moment/column correlations.
One random sign multiplies the entire group's centered field-derived operator
error, rather than assigning independent signs to individual moments.

With B_original bias-correction draws, the factor sqrt(1+1/B_original)
approximates the extra variance of the estimated bootstrap mean. It does not
differentiate the nonlinear bias-correction map. This matches the earlier
experiment's approximation and remains a limitation requiring calibration.

The 512 joint wild/empirical draws estimate uncertainty within this finite
covariance model. Reusing the same covariance pool does not create additional
independent observations. Their quantiles have Monte Carlo error. Changing
64 to 128 covariance draws tests one numerical sensitivity, not validity of
the noise model or absence of reconstruction bias.

Basic diagnostic intervals are gap_observed minus the simulated error
quantiles. The sign fraction is the frequency of a negative gap under local
perturbations; it is NOT a posterior probability that a candidate is correct.
Pointwise 90/95% intervals have no multiple-comparison guarantee. No interval
is used to modify dominance or to report an improved recovery count.

The coefficients, window/bandwidth choices, and training normalizations come
from the observed data and are frozen here. The method describes a local
evaluation sensitivity with those objects held fixed. It does not include
refitting uncertainty, selection effects, random denominators, dependence
between fitted coefficients and other rotations, or common reconstruction
bias. A clean-oracle gap lying outside the interval is therefore evidence
that this conditional noise account is incomplete, not proof that the
reference equation is wrong. Apparent coverage is descriptive on reused data.
