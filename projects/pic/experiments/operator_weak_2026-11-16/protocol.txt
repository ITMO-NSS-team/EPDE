Continuous weak quadrature and operator-risk evaluation, 2026-11-16

Keep the default core-15 datasets, 5% noise seeds 0/1/2, existing finite

No candidate equation integration or new evolutionary search occurs.

Quadrature change:
Use the physical supports selected in the previous train-only PCR experiment.
Map them onto the independently reconstructed full time grid. Interpolate
each pointwise weak monomial locally, piecewise, with degree 3 in time and
degree 5 in space (the shared maximum derivative order plus one). Integrate
that interpolant against the analytically differentiated weak test by Gauss
quadrature. DC tests integrate the represented polynomial exactly; modulated
tests increase Gauss order until relative weight change is <=1e-11. The
accuracy bound and maximum refinement are numerical guards, not statistical
or structural thresholds. Boundary stencils use valid interior nodes only.
This controls quadrature error of the interpolant; it does not guarantee the
interpolant equals the unknown physical field. Remaining data derivatives
use the same fourth-order finite differences as the production weak form.

Fit both ordinary and bias/covariance-corrected least squares on these local
integrals. Reconstruct each bootstrap draw before rebuilding nonlinear weak
terms. Retain signed Gram eigenvalues and the existing numerical rank cutoff.
Compare on the previous frozen spectral score to isolate the fitting change.

Evaluation change:
Use continuous quadrature for the full spectral test bank. Its maximum
frequencies remain the previous training-only LOO field-bandwidth family.
For training weak matrix M, compute bootstrap mean bias, corrected value Z,
and entrywise variance V (including the finite bootstrap-mean variance).
The estimated squared-error difference between retaining and discarding a
moment is 2V-|Z|^2. Normalize each library column by its training mean energy,
sum across the library and both frequency signs, and minimize cumulative risk
over all rectangular frequency prefixes. Every candidate shares the chosen
bank. No residuals, known coefficients, clean fields or held-out groups enter
selection. Cross-row correlation does not change this trace risk, but the
plug-in bias/variance estimates are approximate for nonlinear reconstruction.

Ablations:

Compare ordinary energy and signed cross-product in each bank. The denominator
is training target energy within that common bank, never measurement-noise
variance. Negative cross-products remain signed. Exact coefficients and clean
fields are evaluated only after fitting and bank selection as diagnostics.

Report all equation slots separately. Aggregate counts are descriptive;

Normalization ablation, frozen before the full battery:
The first four seed-0 probes used the observed-energy normalization above.
It can normalize away the very derivative noise amplification being targeted.
The full battery compares that arm to natural operator units: each field is
scaled by its training standard deviation, each coordinate by its observed
domain extent, and derivatives by the inverse extent to their order. Products
and coordinate factors combine those units algebraically. Known opaque
forcing tokens use their RMS amplitude. These are common nondimensionalization
rules, not fitted coefficient magnitudes, clean-field scales or noise variance.
The frequency-prefix risk uses squared units as column scales. Retain both
normalizations as ablations and report all outcomes, including over-smoothing.
results/ contains the initial four probes; unit_results/ is the full battery.
