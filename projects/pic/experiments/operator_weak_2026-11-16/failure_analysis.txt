Why the remaining equations lose after continuous weak fitting
2026-11-16; default core-15 saved systems; 5% noise; seeds 0/1/2

There are seven lost equation-seed cases with the new CLSM fit and old/full
cross-product bank: ac 0/2, forced KdV 0/2, KS 0, nonlinear diffusion 1/2.
They do not have a single cause. The dyad values are frozen; all comparisons
below use a common candidate panel and include registered equivalent laws.

1. Nonlinear diffusion: good coefficients, wrong residual ordering.

The law is u_t = (u_x)^2 + u*u_xx. Continuous weak CLSM reduces median
clean residual energy from 0.37045 to 0.000809. At seed 1, the three training
folds estimate coefficient pairs [1.0058,0.9773], [1.0552,1.0225], and
[1.0139,1.0044], close to [1,1].

Despite this, both drop-one models dominate at seed 1. Dropping (u_x)^2
improves the noisy cross-product by 2.7734 and lowers the dyad by 1.3119,
while WORSENING clean residual energy by 0.14815. Dropping u*u_xx improves
the cross-product by 3.5187 while worsening clean energy by 0.94638.
At seed 2, dropping (u_x)^2 improves the noisy score by 0.16864 while
worsening clean energy by 0.13609. This is a clear discrepancy-ranking error
for these fitted candidates, not evidence that the required term is useless.

The natural-unit bandwidth rule saves seed 2 but not seed 1. Its exact-law
scores are +0.0569, +0.1901, -1.9456, versus clean energy near 8.2e-9.
Reducing column reconstruction error does not guarantee correct ordering of
the cancellation-sensitive residual combination.

2. KS seed 0: very small score gap still removes the correct support.

The fitted coefficients are near [-1,-1,-1]; frozen clean energy is 1.065e-4.
Adding u^2 lowers the noisy score by 4.548e-6 and lowers the dyad from 31.679
to 13.989, while worsening clean energy by 1.518e-6. Exact-law full-bank
cross-products are -0.04359, +0.03342, +0.01325 over the three noise seeds.
The remaining seed-0 dominance thus combines the dyad's preference for this
candidate with a tiny discrepancy-ranking reversal. Half/full fitting
bootstrap agreement does not establish that this gap is statistically real.

Clean-subsample reconstruction itself gives exact-law cross-products around
4.7e-5 to 5.9e-5, above direct clean energy around 4e-11. This is a real
reconstruction contribution, but is much smaller than the noisy scores.
The control does not quantify reconstruction bias under noisy smoothing.

3. Allen-Cahn: small-term estimation and score error both matter.

The diffusion coefficient is 1e-4. At seed 0, the continuous full-model fits
estimate it as 1.48e-4, 4.26e-4, and -4.67e-6 across folds. The drop-diffusion
candidate has lower dyad, lower noisy score (by 1.381e-4), AND lower clean
energy (by 1.231e-4). Improving score precision alone cannot make the present
full-model coefficients beat that competitor on residual accuracy.

At seed 2, two added-term candidates dominate. One improves both noisy and
clean residuals, while the other improves the noisy score but worsens clean
energy. Both estimation error and score-ranking error are present. These
finite-record comparisons do not establish that every unregistered term is
a generally false physical law.

4. Forced KdV: structural survival conceals severely biased coefficients.

The known coefficient vector is [-6,-1,1]. Seed 1 survives only with the
full bank, yet its fitted vectors are approximately [4.01,-0.009,-0.157],
[3.58,-0.066,-0.059], and [3.13,-0.027,-0.148]. The fold cross-products are
+0.425, -8.332, +2.053. Their negative mean protects this support on the
Pareto front despite poor coefficients. That is not successful recovery.

Exact known coefficients receive full-bank scores +1996.4, +1458.8, -1337.7.
Natural-unit risk selection changes these to +617.1, +916.7, +29.2; this
reduces some large magnitudes but does not yield an accurate discrepancy.
In seed 0, several competitors also beat the biased reference on clean
saved-data residual, so fitting and evaluation both need attention.

Post-selection clean controls help localize the issue. The SAME supports,
reconstruction pipeline, integration and linear solves recover approximately
[-6.01,-1.00,1.00] from clean training subsamples. Their clean residual
energies are about 2e-7 to 6e-7; the clean reconstructed exact-law score is
about 3.6e-7. Thus quadrature or missing physical terms alone do not explain
the huge noisy errors. Noise-sensitive reconstruction/fitting remains the
problem. These controls do not distinguish noise-induced smoothing bias
from variance and limited excitation by themselves.

5. Wave: the retention metric hides a fitting regression.

All three seeds retain catalog-valid equations, but median frozen clean
energy of the fitted reference increases from 0.001334 to 0.07763.
The sole coefficient is 0.04. Seed-0 continuous CLSM estimates across folds
are 0.04817, 0.04366, 0.02290, versus old estimates 0.04198, 0.04032, 0.03962.
For the third fold, OLS is also biased (0.02818), and covariance correction
makes it worse. This is not a multicolumn rank-deficiency problem.

With clean fields, continuous fits on these same supports have energies
2.2e-6 to 1.1e-5. Clean-subsample reconstruction leaves those values almost
unchanged. This points to noisy fitting and support choice, not unresolved
integration error, as the principal regression. The supports were selected
for the earlier raw-grid estimator and then reused for controlled comparison;
they have not been re-optimized for this continuous estimator. That mismatch
is a plausible contributor to test next, not a proven complete explanation.

6. Why the operator-risk bank is not enough.

The bank minimizes estimated mean-square error summed over normalized weak
library columns. For corrected training matrix Z and estimated entrywise
variance V, the retain-minus-discard risk is 2V-|Z|^2. This is a principled
projection-risk calculation under the plug-in noise model, and its discrete
implementation passes finite-noise and brute-force checks.

However, an equation residual is a coefficient-weighted combination of those
columns. Its error depends on cross-column covariance and cancellation.
Pairwise candidate score differences add shared-data dependence. Minimizing
columnwise trace risk is therefore not the same target as minimizing error
in a residual-score comparison. The current rule also only searches prefixes
within the old field-selected maximum frequencies and retains DC. It is an
exploratory bank restriction, not a universal uncertainty-optimal score.

Next diagnostic:
Estimate uncertainty of paired residual-score differences with operators and
candidate scores resampled together, keeping splits and dyad comparable.
Account for common reconstruction bias and dependence across rotations;
three rotations are not three independent observations. Independently test
training-only support selection against the continuous estimator in wave and
small-diffusion cases. Do not select a significance cutoff, complexity
penalty, or per-system window from these clean diagnostics.
