Target-invariant weak fitting and held-out discrepancy

Question. Within a fixed equation slot, does an unordered set of weak columns define the same fitted equation and ranking regardless of which term is written on the left? Does removing the preferred target preserve useful structural discrimination? Constants remain ordinary deterministic columns. No term-count objective or penalty.

Evaluation. All 15 default systems at 5% iid noise. Replay archived seed 21, then test measurement seeds 6101, 6102 and 6103 on the same benchmark trajectories. Use the three disjoint raw groups, frozen common weak geometry, reconstructed training fields, whitening and held-out noise corrections of the matched earlier panel. Treat the established physical weak-bank layout and candidate slots as fixed inputs to every method. The historical geometry was designed on old benchmark data. This is a matched fixed-panel sensitivity study, independent in noise on the new seeds.

Write each candidate as a homogeneous law Mv=0 including its former target as an ordinary column. Fit an implicit direction on the training group's local weak matrix. Divide each column by its Euclidean norm and take the right singular vector for the smallest singular value, with real coefficients (stack real and imaginary rows for complex inputs). Undo the column scaling. A numerically nonunique least singular direction or a zero training scale means unresolved. A one-column candidate is one direction and is retained. Fix projective sign only for representation and compare projective angles for validation. Exchanging units or moving terms across the equality with the same physical law must preserve the fit up to scale and the score up to roundoff.

Validation discrepancy. Average the cross-product of held-out weak residuals from two groups, within each training rotation divided by the sum of the squared weighted pilot-column norms times squared corresponding coefficients. This scale is common to all orientations of a law. It has no coefficient-sign cancellation. A score near zero alone does not identify unique coefficients. Exact constants are included and the known additive phase and energy identities of ode_simple are checked separately.

Ablations. Compare the old fitting/score/dyad, old fitted coefficients with only the new denominator (these fits still depend on target), the new implicit fit and symmetric score with old dyad, and the fully orientation-independent variant. For the latter compute the legacy dyadic coefficient-instability score with every term serving as pivot, and take the median across pivots. Use a common partition width from the archived panel. This retains the original slab logic without selecting the most favorable orientation. The fixed historical dyad in the other arms remains target dependent. Also compute the old-coefficient symmetric-score arm with symmetric dyad to distinguish effects.

Judge registered-law and exact-reference Pareto survival and front sizes, counting registered equivalents. If a candidate is unresolved do not silently remove it: give conservative lower and upper bounds for slot retention by allowing unknown candidates to dominate or survive. Report known-only front sizes separately. Record orientation/unit numerical replay and scale-independent angular error on registered coefficient vectors using frozen clean local-column norms only after model outputs freeze. Survival and coefficient accuracy are different endpoints. No dynamical solves or production changes.
