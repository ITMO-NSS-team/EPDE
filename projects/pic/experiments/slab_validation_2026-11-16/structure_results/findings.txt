Kepler: structure and mechanism of the surviving dominators
2026-11-16

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
The ten archived Kepler candidates that dominate after CLSM coefficient
correction are four distinct algebraic forms. The same ten survive in all
three noise seeds, raw and smoothed stages, with ordinary and crossed weak
discrepancy. Six entries are redundant spellings of the four forms.

The four families are repeatable, approximate descriptions of this particular
orbit. Their structural mismatch is strongly attenuated by the weak window,
and their residuals are less sensitive to measurement noise than the reference
inverse-force balance. Dyad measures coefficient repeatability, so these
approximations can beat the reference on both existing objectives.

Canonical structures
--------------------
Reference: u'' = -1/u^2 + 0.6/u^3.
Coefficients a, b, c below are independent fitted constants in each family.

A (1 entry): u' = (a*sin(omega*t) + b*cos(omega*t))/u^2
  archive_0
B (3 entries): u' = a + (b + c*cos(omega*t))/u^2
  archive_1, archive_3, archive_4
C (1 entry): u' = (a*sin(omega*t) + b*cos(omega*t))/u
  archive_2
D (5 entries): u' = a*u + (b + c*cos(omega*t))/u^2
  archive_6, archive_8, archive_9, archive_10, archive_13

Examples of exact aliases: u*uinv3 = u^-2; u^3*uinv3 = 1;
u*uinv = 1; u^3*uinv2 = u; u^2*uinv = u. Maximum discrepancy/instability
difference within equivalent groups was 2.37e-14 across all seeds/stages.
Deduplication changes the count from ten to four; it does not restore the
reference's nondominance.

1. They encode the observed orbit's fundamental
-----------------------------------------------
The analytic benchmark frequency is 1.23323761; the fitted seed-0 frequency
is 1.23318873 (about 0.004% difference). This is an orbital signal, unlike
the Cauchy high-frequency shared-noise carrier found in the earlier analysis.

For A, z=u^3 gives z'=3*a*sin(omega*t)+3*b*cos(omega*t), so z is a constant
plus a sinusoid. The clean u^3 record is within 2.35% centered-relative RMS
of such a sinusoid. Family C similarly describes u^2 as a sinusoid plus a
constant. B and D add constant/state contributions to the periodic forcing.
These transformations explain the forms; they are not new scoring rules.

2. The weak window hides much of the approximation error
-------------------------------------------------------
The current window has support 5.1234 time units, about one orbital period
(5.0949). Its amplitude gains at the first three orbital harmonics are
0.5565, 0.0390, 0.0104. Higher harmonics are strongly attenuated.

Clean-oracle fits evaluated with the SAME coefficients pointwise and weakly:

           Pointwise WAPE      Weak WAPE
A               4.404%           0.279%
B               5.894%           0.409%
C              29.712%           2.041%
D               4.443%           0.278%

These are measured residual changes, not an assumption about which harmonics
the residual contains. Pointwise coefficients refitted by least squares still
leave nonzero errors. The reference has pointwise relative L2 error 1.64e-15
when refitted with exact derivatives; the alternatives are not exact analytic
equivalents, even on this orbit. On the clean weak data the reference is also
far more accurate (WAPE 0.001264%).

Weak residuals and pointwise residuals have different target normalizations;
their comparison diagnoses the effect of the weak pipeline, not a universal
conversion factor between errors or an estimate of trajectory error.

3. Noise reverses that clean-data advantage
------------------------------------------
First-order propagation of the known iid measurement noise through the SAME
weak pipeline gives the following expected noise-only contribution to WAPE:
reference 3.890%; A 0.707%; B 0.736%; C 0.465%; D 0.728%.

This calculation includes shared target/RHS noise. It holds coefficients and
frequency fixed and omits nonlinear bias and estimation uncertainty. It is an
oracle sensitivity diagnostic, not a calibrated noise-normalized objective.

The reference sensitivity comes predominantly from its inverse-power RHS:
target noise RMS 0.00126, RHS noise RMS 0.01394. Thus derivative order alone
does not explain the result. For f(u)=-u^-2+0.6*u^-3,
f'(u)=2*u^-3-1.8*u^-4; small radius errors can strongly perturb the force.
The two legitimate force contributions also substantially cancel. Correcting
coefficient bias cannot remove measurement error in the scored residual.

4. Dyad rewards repetition of these approximate relations
--------------------------------------------------------
The current K7 configuration yields only two dyad slabs here. Each spans
about 2.39 orbital periods in weak-row centres. Both therefore see repeated
instances of the same periodic waveform. A stable approximation to that
waveform can have very repeatable coefficients while remaining inaccurate.

For example, family A's two coefficient fits are
(0.131327, 0.490536) and (0.132260, 0.492041), giving I=0.00507.
Reference slab fits are (-1.001626, 0.602464) and (-0.988376, 0.585903),
giving I=0.02059. Their greater fluctuation is consistent with the measured
noise sensitivity of the force balance.

The slabs' raw weak supports overlap by 4.8032 time units, nearly one period.
They are not independent raw-data replicates. This is an additional limitation;
the analysis does not isolate overlap as the cause of dominance.

An algebraic illustration of what dyad does not measure
-----------------------------------------------------
Seed-0 D becomes, with z=u^3:
  z' = 0.331917*z - 0.428814 + 1.579381*cos(omega*t).
Perturbations in z satisfy delta_z'=0.331917*delta_z, an e-folding time of
3.013. Low dyad I therefore does not establish dynamical stability or accurate
trajectory predictions. This follows algebraically without solving a candidate
ODE. Positive growth is not a universal rejection criterion: genuine systems
can also have it.

Implication
-----------
The diagnosis supports the concern about precision: coefficient repeatability
alone cannot certify a correct equation. But simply adding the present weak
residual does not resolve it, because the window suppresses structural mismatch
while the reference retains a larger measurement-noise contribution. The two
objectives can jointly prefer a repeatable approximation to one observed orbit.
Cross-reconstruction removes one shared-noise mechanism but cannot eliminate
these real-signal approximations. No replacement objective is validated here.

Reproducibility and limits
--------------------------

No candidate ODE/PDE was integrated. The diagnostic uses the existing record
and the known benchmark's analytic derivatives. Algebraic eccentric-anomaly
inversion reproduces the saved clean radius to 5.28e-12 maximum absolute error.
Analytic measurement Jacobians match directional finite differences to less
than 1e-8 relative error. No production objective or keep rule was changed.
Results cover these archived Kepler competitors, not every search candidate.
