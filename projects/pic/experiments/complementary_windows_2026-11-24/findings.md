# A second weak window on 14 transfer systems

The existing selector and the complementary selector recovered a registered law in exactly the same 218 of 228 equation-slot observations. Keep the existing default on the evidence of this comparison.

The 14 scalar and coupled ODE/PDE systems each supplied one trajectory and 12 archived 5% measurement-noise draws. Forced KdV motivated the design and was scored separately. Recovery means that a registered equation or equivalent from the fixed candidate panel is on the held-out-error and training-dyad Pareto front. The experiment measures ranking given the terms. Every fit used the same row count in both arms, and all candidates for a given arm used the same geometry.

| Transfer outcome | Existing selector | Up to one complementary geometry |

| Registered law, first six draws | 109/114 | 109/114 |

| CPU seconds after loading the shared data and plan | 5.77 | 10.86 |

The first training perturbation proposed a second geometry on 27 of 168 recordings. Seven proposals also improved the number of resolved column combinations on the verification perturbation. They changed 354 of 26,172 fitting rows. Burgers and Lotka–Volterra each used a second geometry on multiple draws. Navier–Stokes and the division PDE each used one once. Across the 19 slots, three coefficient spreads fell, three rose and thirteen stayed the same within numerical tolerance. Baseline WAPE, dyad and rank-deficiency status were checked against the archived panel.

Forced KdV accepted one second geometry in twelve draws. Both arms retained the registered law on two of the twelve. This panel supplies the catalogued forcing term. Its result concerns candidate ranking with that term available.

The strict count criterion changes only when a new combination of columns crosses the native signal-to-noise boundary. A decrease in uncertainty for an already measurable derivative can leave the count unchanged. The results support testing uncertainty of individual coefficients or weakly determined column combinations as a separate selection criterion. They provide no demonstrated recovery advantage for this prototype.
