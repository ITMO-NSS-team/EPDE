Shared intrinsic KdV coefficients across a forced and an unforced trajectory
18 November 2026

The existing unforced PDE-FIND record supplies information about the nonlinear and dispersive coefficients that was missing on the forced record. With forcing-sensitive measurements on the forced trajectory, the two datasets together also resolved forcing under independent and negatively correlated noise. Under positive noise correlation all four forced observations admitted zero forcing even at the exact benchmark intrinsic coefficients.

Input selection. The registered forced field contains 81 by 81 samples. The 101 by 101 field in projects/benchmarking/KdV/Data/KdV_sln_100.csv and its wSINDy copy agree exactly with the registered field on their 21 by 21 common coordinate grid. The checked EPDE sibling copies of kdv/data.csv match the registered array pointwise. These files do not establish a different forced trajectory. The separate registered kdv data in kdv_sindy.mat has a different trajectory and specifies the same intrinsic coefficients (-6, -1) with external input identically zero. Domains and trajectory conditions differ as well as the input.

Primary tests. Three matched iid seed pairs (51 to 53) reuse six previously generated 5%-noise fields. Local and broad weak regions were calibrated separately for each record, reserving a nominal 2.5% per-record error budget for pairing. The recorded physical equation was compatible on all pairs. Each pair excluded the dispersion-only model even when its coefficient was free, every fit without the nonlinear term, and every fit without dispersion. The unforced record alone supplied these exclusions. The forced record alone admitted the locked -1/5 rival in all three cases. All three paired fits still admitted zero forcing with the original local and broad readouts.

Directional follow-up. The forced study used three weak banks and their projections onto the known forcing waveform, with a simultaneous four-column calibration over the whole support. Pair its 12 archived observations (seeds 4705–4708 in the three noise profiles) with each of the three independent-noise observations on the unforced trajectory. Each record uses its 126th of 128 bootstrap per-draw maxima to account for testing two trajectories. The six-readout forced records alone admitted both a zero-forcing fit and a dispersion-only fit in all 12. Every paired physical equation was compatible and all 36 paired fits excluded every dispersion-only coefficient. Forcing deletion was excluded on each of the four iid and four negatively correlated forced records against every unforced partner. Zero forcing remained feasible on each of the four positively correlated records against every partner. There are 12 distinct forced fields and three unforced fields in this comparison, not 36 independent trials. Three initial full-support automatic witness searches were inconclusive although the physical control passed. An additional observed-data interior-witness search located finite fits in all three, verified against the original inequalities.

Positive-noise diagnosis. Keeping the benchmark intrinsic coefficients fixed and setting forcing to zero permits a direct comparison on the same clean forced trajectory. For each record, divide the squared clean weak residual by the estimated noise allowance in each bank, then take the largest ratio. A ratio above one exceeds the cutoff. The ranges were 1.77–5.14 for iid, 0.46–0.77 for positive, and 14.49–23.55 for negative correlation. Even the clean omitted-forcing discrepancy falls below the estimated allowance in all four positive cases. This diagnoses the current test's precision and does not establish whether the allowance is statistically optimal.

These are solver-free, retrospective and conditional on the recorded inputs, two benchmark trajectories and the shared-coefficient assumption. Positive-definite exclusion certificates and finite witnesses were checked with rational arithmetic on the stored binary64 measurements, covariances and cutoffs. No error-rate guarantee for adaptive discovery follows from these bootstrap thresholds. Term count plays no role.

Priority for independent confirmation: hold the positive correlation, benchmark forcing and shared intrinsic coefficients fixed. Compare one observation with several independent measurement repeats on the same forced field, averaging corrected weak moments before fitting. Recalibrate the entire averaged procedure and test both physical-law retention and exclusion of all zero-forcing refits. A fresh trajectory with a separately observed forcing pattern would then test transfer beyond these two trajectories.
