Completed 2026-11-17: solver-free uncertainty from raw weak residual moments

Direct weak moments with an estimated iid noise model give near95% average
coverage on the default benchmark, including an independent fresh-seed
confirmation. This is a useful improvement over coefficient-contrast
bootstrap uncertainty. A single-coefficient confidence-set inversion also
works for wave. It does not establish confidence after equation search,
generic colored-noise calibration, or a replacement for dyad in ranking.

What was tested

The primary study covers all15 default systems at fresh anchors24/25/26:
45 records,60 reference equation/anchor cases and93 registered cases with
equivalents. It uses256 calibration,256 independent bootstrap-check and256
actual benchmark measurements per record:34,560 field evaluations.
Both local and broad native-grid moment banks are fixed before outcomes.
Local geometry comes from historical seed0 base_plan; broad geometry uses
the common interior and frequencies0,+/-1,+/-2 per axis. Remaining factor
derivatives use existing fourth-order central differences; native integration
uses degree3 time/degree5 spatial interpolation and analytic test derivatives.

Ordinary raw and Gaussian-contraction corrected moments are compared. At a
fixed coefficient vector v, the residual is M*v. The statistic averages
squared residual errors divided by estimated marginal noise variance.
Its cutoff comes from shared-field bootstrap draws, preserving dependence
between overlapping windows. No independent-row or chi-square approximation,
no coefficient-level denominator, no covariance ridge and no noisy Gram
inverse enter this uncertainty test. Coefficients are not fitted inside it.

The primary noise model is the previous estimated separable AR model.
Its GCV reconstructed field supplies simulated noise covariance, not an
empirical fitted-coefficient bias correction. All method inputs freeze
before clean residuals/exact coefficient queries are evaluated.
Known supports are used to evaluate calibration. There is no new structural
search or claim about confidence after data-dependent support selection.

Noise-model repair and fresh confirmation

The benchmark injects iid Gaussian noise at a common relative amplitude
across variables. The flexible AR estimator sometimes infers strong
negative correlations on these iid data. Example ODE24: estimated rho=-0.336,
sigma0.0893 versus actual0.1030; the bootstrap/actual weak-residual SD ratio
is about0.623. Small systematic scale errors also matter when many local
moments make the score distribution narrow.

The follow-up explicitly uses the known iid noise FAMILY, but estimates
the amplitude from noisy observations, without using the true5% value.
Observed-anchor standard deviations define variable units. High-order
differences, with frozen previously selected orders, estimate a pooled
relative variance after division by binomial(2k,k). Both the noise family
and amplitude estimator change relative to the AR study; the experiment
does not attribute the entire improvement to correlation restriction alone.

Two versions use identical geometry and fields:
  iid_fixed: retain the anchor's estimated amplitude in every draw.
  iid_studentized: re-estimate amplitude and polynomial correction in each
  measurement/draw, then divide each error by its own amplitude estimate.
  Calibration variance and check quantiles use those same transformed errors.
No arbitrary inflation factor is fitted. The estimated correction with a
random amplitude is not claimed exactly unbiased; mean shifts are measured.

All45 paired records are rerun. The identical methods are then confirmed
on ALL15 systems at fresh anchors27/28/29, another45 records, with disjoint
actual-measurement and bootstrap seeds. Each again has256/256/256 draws.
The paired follow-up reuses original actual measurements; it is not a new
independent replication. The fresh cohort was specified before its outcomes.

Actual coefficient-region inversion: wave

For wave's fixed reference support u_tt = beta*u_xx, invert the
iid-studentized residual test over beta, with no sign restriction or
clean-coefficient centering. Both banks and all six anchors are reported.
Parameterization beta=tan(theta) examines the whole real line, including
the infinite-coefficient limit. Two grid resolutions8193/16385 plus
data-derived OLS angles give agreeing roots; scalar boundary refinement
uses tolerance1e-12. This is numerical one-parameter inversion, not a proof
against arbitrarily narrow missed components or a multicoefficient projection.
It does not integrate an ODE/PDE.

All12 regions are single finite intervals and contain the exact beta0.04.
This inclusion rate on six anchors is not itself a95% calibration estimate.
Example fresh seed27:
  Local: [0.02682156, 0.05409908]
  Broad: [0.03719576, 0.04465890]
Across all six anchors, mean widths are0.028672 local and0.008354 broad.
On the256 actual repeated measurements per anchor, exact-coefficient
acceptance is94.92% local and95.96% broad. Those fields were already used
in the moment study; this checks inversion consistency, not new replication.
The narrower broad interval gives a concrete precision comparison for
this support. It does not imply broad moments universally identify models.

Decision

Keep dyad's current structural role. Use these direct weak-moment tests as
an experimental uncertainty layer under an explicitly specified noise
family. A small uncertainty-normalized residual must not become a new
ranking preference for weakly identified or overparameterized equations.

This round supplies tested code for moment regions and a one-parameter
coefficient inversion. It does not yet supply honest uncertainty after
evolutionary search, general colored-noise handling, or joint coefficient
regions for forced KdV/KS. The next useful tests are multicoefficient set
inversion/false-model exclusion and correlated-noise calibration, retaining
the simpler iid-fixed control. Do not promote a generic production default
or per-system bank selector from these results.
