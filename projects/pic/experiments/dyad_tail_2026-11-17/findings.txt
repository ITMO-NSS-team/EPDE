Why a small coefficient causes nonregular dyad uncertainty
2026-11-17

The production statistic has a precise counterexample to a general
variance-based uncertainty interpretation. Take a single constant feature,
16 observations split into two8-row slabs, no intercept, and target data
consisting of iid Gaussian measurement noise. The true coefficient is zero.
Let the two fitted slab coefficients be c1 and c2. Their median is
(c1+c2)/2, and their median absolute deviation is |c1-c2|/2. Hence

  I = |c1-c2| / |c1+c2|.

For independent equal-variance zero-mean Gaussian c1,c2, the rotated sum
and difference are independent equal-variance Gaussians. Their absolute
ratio has the absolute-Cauchy distribution:

  P(I > x) = (2/pi)*atan(1/x), x>0.

Its median is1; its95th percentile is12.7062. The uncapped model has an
infinite mean and no finite variance. Production code caps exact division
failures at the largest finite float, so the literal capped implementation
is finite; the model explains the near-zero-denominator pathology, not a
claim that every real candidate follows this distribution exactly.

Production verification uses its actual minimum-row rule and stable slab
solver. Across256 synthetic noise records, the maximum scaled difference
from the formula is1.84e-14. Reducing noise from sigma1 to sigma1e-8 leaves
the median dyad unchanged at0.96525 for those paired records. At EXACTLY
zero noise the implementation returns0. Thus the score at a zero
coefficient does not converge to the noise-free score as noise decreases.
This is a lack of continuity in the statistical limit, not merely a need
for more bootstrap draws or higher numerical precision.

The nonzero constant control behaves correctly: with true coefficient1.3
and sigma1e-8, median dyad is1.16e-9. Constants are not the special problem;
the same ratio issue can arise for any near-zero coefficient. No constant
or identity is removed by this test.

An independent1,048,576-pair Gaussian simulation yields quantiles
0.99957,6.28250,12.65259,63.67184 at50/90/95/99%, agreeing with the analytic
values1,6.31375,12.70620,63.65674. Across independent128-draw batches the
sample SD has median12.69 and99.9th percentile10,434. Quantiles of the
score remain meaningful, but its apparent sample SD can be dominated by
rare denominator events. This does not by itself establish valid bootstrap
coverage for a latent physical stability parameter.

Both transformed statistics control the magnitude of extreme samples,

CONCLUSION

Dyad can be useful as a ranking rule and an implicit preference against
unsupported small coefficients. That is different from measuring physical
coefficient variation with a reliably estimable variance. The fresh parent
experiment also ties dyad with complexity at54/60 registered retention, so
its structural benefit should be reassessed against that simpler control.

A future uncertainty-aware alternative should compare coefficient contrasts
with their sampling uncertainty, and keep support/complexity preference
explicit, rather than divide by a coefficient median approaching zero.
This is a proposed direction, not a tested replacement. The existing
prediction-space replacement already lost structural discrimination; merely
removing a denominator is not enough. No universal production change is
justified by the present evidence.
