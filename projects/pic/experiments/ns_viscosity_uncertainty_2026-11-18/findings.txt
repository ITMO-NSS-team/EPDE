Fresh benchmark evidence

The refitted median difference D(reduced) − D(full) was +6.76e−5, with an empirical central repeat range from +1.36e−5 to +1.19e−4. The two-sided exact binomial 95% interval for the fraction of future wins on this trajectory is 97.2% to 100%. With the physical coefficients fixed in both models and only the tested viscosity removed, the median difference was +2.07e−4, also positive in 128/128. Allowing other coefficients to adjust absorbed about two thirds of the median difference. The median fitted viscosity was 0.00994 against 0.01000 in the benchmark.

In the earlier Allen–Cahn test, diffusion was favored in 41/128 fresh fields and none of the six observation-specific ranges excluded zero. In Navier–Stokes, all six original ranges excluded zero in favor of retention. These fixed-pair results provide contrasting examples of the same rule at the same noise percentage. They do not quantify general discovery or pruning accuracy.

Calibration of the original bootstrap

All 768 bootstrap margins were positive. Each of the six forecasts overstated the typical difference, with bootstrap medians from 1.44e−4 to 2.52e−4. On the first prospective cohort the six nominal 95% ranges covered 37, 0, 29, 6, 9 and 23 of the same 128 fields. Every miss was below the corresponding lower endpoint. A correct sign decision can coexist with poor numerical coverage.

Crossed measurements

With coefficients from one half of the fresh Navier–Stokes fields and validation from the other half, 99.4% to 99.5% of the crossed margins favored the full law. Swapping coefficient and validation donors in the same way on Allen–Cahn gave 24.6% to 46.2%. Each combination reuses inputs, so these cross-products do not increase the number of independent fields.

An exploratory swap examined why the generated margins were too large. Using fresh coefficient and validation donors gave a mean Navier–Stokes difference of 6.64e−5. With the same fresh coefficient donors, bootstrap validation raised that mean to 1.37e−4 through 1.93e−4 across the six anchors. With the original fresh validation and bootstrap coefficients, all six means instead fell below 6.64e−5. For this contrast the upward shift followed the generated validation matrices and their scaling. These swaps aggregate the effects of the assumed signal, measurement noise, observation correction, whitening and normalization; they cannot attribute the shift to one of those ingredients in isolation.
