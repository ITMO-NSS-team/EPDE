Temporal information and correlation-aware reconstruction
=========================================================

The local quintic contrasts remove localized signal effectively, but they also
remove low frequencies needed to identify positive AR1 correlation. Neither a
small optimizer score nor a near-correct median sigma rules out an unidentified
upper tail. Studentization is particularly sensitive: if bootstrap sigma/rho
drift toward high-amplitude, near-unit-root noise, their covariance metrics can
grow enough to make the calibrated normalized statistic misleadingly small.

The temporal method uses the known time axis0 globally. It fits the already
verified AR2-curvature/AR1-observation-noise likelihood to temporal second
differences only, pooling spatial lines. Four fixed starts and a verified state
score replace numerical gradients. Statistical dependence between lines means
the pooled likelihood is composite for parameter uncertainty; no likelihood
Hessian confidence claim is made.

For spatial rho, fourth temporal differences suppress smooth signal while
preserving the spatial covariance factor of separable Gaussian noise. If
Cov(e)=sigma^2 Rt tensor Rs, then a temporal filter D gives
Cov(De)=sigma^2 (D Rt D') tensor Rs. Thus the spatial correlation is unchanged
at a fixed time. After pooled median centering, estimate adjacent sign agreement
tau and use rho=sin(pi*tau/2), the Gaussian sign-correlation identity. The sign
score has bounded influence, but remaining temporal signal can still bias it.
No arbitrary signals or nonseparable noise identification theorem is asserted.
No estimate uses injected sigma/rho or clean signal. Noise-only fits and sigma
ratios are evaluation diagnostics after raw fits are frozen.

The AC bootstrap diagnostic identifies another model mismatch: the nuisance
signal is reconstructed by ordinary pooled GCV, which treats errors along each
axis as independent. Positive correlation can therefore be retained as signal.
Generating fresh noise around this contaminated pilot adds noise twice in some
directions. Improving the subsequent noise fit cannot alone remove this source
of bootstrap mismatch.

For an axis with estimated covariance R=L L' and existing roughness penalty K,
write y=L z and mu=L f. The penalized problem becomes
  min_f ||z-f||^2 + lambda f' L' K L f.
Diagonalize L' K L=U diag(d) U'; use the same pooled GCV shrinkage on U' L^-1 y,
then map back with L U. The amplitude sigma cancels from the smoothing choice.
For time, K is the original natural-cubic-spline penalty with a two-dimensional
affine nullspace. For spatial axes it is the original DCT fourth-power penalty
with a constant nullspace. Reconstruction still proceeds axis by axis.

At rho0 the implementation agrees with the existing method. Added affine time
trends, constants and changes of units are preserved in the checks. Additional
checks compare with the direct normal equations (I+lambda R K) mu=y at several
positive/negative correlations and finite lambda values. This algebra is signal
smoothing, not integration of a discovered dynamical equation.

Coverage studies have distinct scopes. The69-anchor local6 study and its
studentized follow-up use the original nuisance pilot. The69 fresh temporal
parameter cases plus192 bootstrap fields assess noise estimation, not coverage.
The paired AC pilot screen changes only reconstruction on already seen anchors.
Only the targeted AC-positive coverage study on fresh seeds48..50 tests the
combined temporal estimator/correlation-aware pilot with fixed, correction-refit
and studentized uncertainty. Its result cannot establish universal15-system
validity. No per-system estimator switching or tuned coverage thresholds occur.

All actual-field replicates retain the anchor's nuisance pilot and weak design.
Noise estimation is repeated, and studentization repeats the noise-dependent
metric, but reconstruction itself is not repeated for every actual dataset.
The reported operator coverage therefore evaluates conditional calibration
given that pilot/design. It is not unconditional confidence coverage of a full
pipeline that reconstructs a new pilot on every dataset. That additional layer
of nuisance-estimation uncertainty remains a separate validation requirement.
