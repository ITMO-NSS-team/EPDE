Fresh pilot-correction and main-refit uncertainty study
Started 2026-11-16; completed 2026-11-17.

Completed all15 systems with three fresh anchor noise seeds18/19/20 at5%:
45 records,60 equation/seed cases,957 unregistered-competitor/reference
pairs,17,280 measurement replicates and1,209,600 main candidate/fold refits.
All work is solver-free. No production edits or commits.

The new result changes the emphasis: refitting main coefficients matters,
but the proposed pilot bias correction is not a universal fix. Discrepancy
error ranges remain comparatively well calibrated in aggregate, while
coefficient errors in forced KdV and dyad errors remain unreliable.
Fresh structural results tie dyad with the complexity control; they do not
confirm dyad's one-case advantage from the earlier reused-panel comparison.

DESIGN AND SCOPE

Each system's historical seed0 fixes the full candidate panel, weak geometry,
validation geometry, and each candidate's joint-risk-selected fitting bank
and OLS/CLSM estimator. Each new anchor rebuilds its reconstruction, noise
estimates, coefficient corrections and validation weights using noisy data.
This is a frozen-design replication, not a fresh evolutionary search or
reselection of all fitting options. It differs from the earlier three-seed
screen, whose bank/method choices were made separately on those old records.

The standard pilot is the existing GCV reconstruction. The alternative is
2*S(y)-S(S(y)), using the same linear smoother S fitted to the anchor. This
introduces no free smoothing parameter. For a fixed S its deterministic
bias operator is -(I-S)^2 instead of -(I-S); that algebra does not guarantee
better finite-sample reconstruction or inference after S was learned.
The alternative changes only the bootstrap pilot, not the main anchor fit.

For each anchor,128 standard-pilot and128 alternative-pilot measurements
share the SAME random innovations. Another128 independent measurements use
the actual clean field and known5% iid Gaussian noise. Clean controls enter
only after designs, fits, pilots, noise models and corrections are frozen.

Each field produces the existing full-record dyad and two discrepancy
versions. Fixed D holds main coefficients fixed. Refit D reconstructs all
three training groups again, including GCV reselection, then refits the main
coefficients using their frozen bank/method and mean/Gram corrections.
Noise is shared across rotations and both objectives. Validation weights,
correction maps, geometry and method choice remain fixed. This is still
conditional uncertainty, not the full discovery/selection pipeline.

Centers are the corresponding noise-free pilot or clean functional, with
observation-noise corrections off for noise-free refitting/validation.
They are not assumed to equal the physical reference equation. Independently
stored clean residual energies describe coefficient accuracy. A retained
registered structure is not automatically an accurate coefficient fit.

CALIBRATION

Mean coverage of nominal95% empirical error ranges, standard -> corrected
pilot (averaged over957 dependent candidate pairs):

  Fixed-coefficient discrepancy       93.94% -> 93.96%
  Refitted-coefficient discrepancy    93.14% -> 93.64%
  Dyad                               69.76% -> 70.19%

Giving each of the60 equation/seed cases equal weight yields93.91/93.93%,
92.90/93.45%, and69.89/69.87%, respectively. The weighting difference does
not change the conclusion. The discrepancy result is promising as a
conditional diagnostic, not a simultaneous or post-selection guarantee.
128 draws offer limited resolution, particularly for rare fit failures.

System-specific changes contradict a universal pilot fix:

  Forced KdV refit-D coverage          67.78% ->79.51%
  Forced KdV dyad coverage             79.67% ->92.22%
  KS dyad coverage                    71.81% ->39.44%
  Wave refit-D coverage               90.18% ->86.72%
  Wave dyad coverage                  49.06% ->44.65%
  AC dyad coverage                    87.82% ->93.71%

The correction reduces observed field L2 error in only27/60 variable/anchor
comparisons. Neither field error nor its improvement is a sufficient
criterion for derivative-sensitive score calibration. Do not select the
pilot per system after seeing these clean outcomes.

MAIN COEFFICIENTS CANNOT GENERALLY BE FROZEN IN UNCERTAINTY

The actual repeated-noise SD ratio, refit versus fixed D, has median1.754,
90th percentile15.36 and99th percentile9752.8 across957 paired differences.
Some ratios are below1: refitting changes dependence as well as variance.
Huge ratios can reflect a very small fixed-fit paired SD and rare refit
outliers; they are not stable universal inflation factors.

For the reference equation alone in forced KdV seed18, SD rises from0.007698
to0.097333, a12.64-fold change. Across all forced-KdV competitor differences
the median ratio is770.6, a different and more extreme statistic. Main
refits should be included rather than approximated by an arbitrary common
variance multiplier.

Reference coefficient error-range coverage averages88.52% with the standard
pilot and88.76% with the corrected one (459 fold/coefficient/anchor cases).
Forced KdV is particularly bad:8.56% ->5.47%. Wave improves70.49% ->83.51%.
Most other systems lie around88--99%. Good aggregate residual-discrepancy
coverage does not imply calibrated physical coefficient uncertainty.

The post-hoc fit replay explains part of the forced-KdV tail. For seed18,
the third training fold's corrected local fit has normalized inverse
sensitivity8.02 in the median-error replicate, versus2112.2 in the worst.
The corrected matrix has a negative eigenvalue in both. The worst reference
clean energy is1.3232, versus median0.1110 and anchor0.1188. These diagnostics
identify a sensitive corrected fit; they do not establish that every error
comes from the same mechanism or justify a tuned ridge.

Across each403,200-refit ensemble, indefinite corrected normal equations
occur3645 times for standard-pilot draws,3263 for corrected-pilot draws,
and6000 for benchmark draws. Rank-deficient fits occur12,288 times in each
ensemble, confined to panels for AC, inviscid Burgers, NS and wave. These
are explicit diagnostics of the historical signed/minimum-norm fitting
rule, not silently dropped fits. All arrays remain finite and every case
is retained in the reported summaries. No fallback estimator was introduced.

STRUCTURAL CONTROL: DYAD VERSUS COMPLEXITY

Both retain a registered equation in54/60 fresh anchor cases, with exactly
the same failures: all three AC and all three forced-KdV anchors. With
actual repeated noise and refitted main coefficients:

  Criterion       Mean registered retention    Mean Pareto front size
  Dyad                      92.53%                       3.240
  Feature count             92.83%                       2.819

With main coefficients fixed, the corresponding figures are90.04%/3.097
and90.48%/2.743. These dependent repeated-measurement rates are not new
discovery success rates. Unregistered candidates are not automatically
false laws. Smaller fronts alone do not prove greater scientific precision.

The earlier56/60 versus55/60 advantage for dyad on reused records therefore
does not generalize into a clear advantage here. Keep complexity as a
serious matched baseline. No full-search replacement is justified yet,
and no calibrated confidence interpretation should be attached to dyad.

FOLLOW-UP AND PRACTICAL DIRECTION

The separately frozen dyad_tail_2026-11-17 follow-up derives and verifies
a zero-coefficient ratio pathology, then tests monotone transformations on
these same saved arrays. Raw, log1p and bounded dyad error-range coverages
are69.76%,65.97% and49.85% with the standard pilot. All candidate orders
are preserved, so this is not another replacement discovery experiment.
Tail compression alone does not repair inference. See its findings.txt
for the exact two-slab derivation and the finite-cap qualification.

The defensible next formulation separates structural preference from
uncertainty: use dyad and complexity as ranking controls, assess precision
with paired discrepancy including main refits, and investigate coefficient
changes against their sampling uncertainty without dividing by an estimated
coefficient level. Such a criterion must preserve feature-unit invariance,
account for correlated weak moments, and face a fresh full-panel comparison.
It has NOT been validated by this study. The current heterogeneity helper's
ridge and unresolved-level guard are not silently promoted as that fix.

Constants and registered equivalents remain present. The problem concerns
coefficients near zero, not an additive constant being intrinsically wrong.
No ban on ODE_simple's valid phase/energy identities is introduced.
