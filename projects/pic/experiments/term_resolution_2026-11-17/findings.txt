Solver-free term resolution in EPDE
17 November 2026

The question is whether the measured weak residuals require a particular term. For each deletion, every surviving coefficient is free to change. A compatible deletion means that the data admit at least one reduced equation under the stated weak constraints. The physical equation can still contain that term. Equation size contributes nothing to the score, feasibility test or choice of a winning structure.

1. Certificate results on the default systems

The 69 archived noise fields comprise 15 systems under iid noise and four systems under both signs of correlation, with three seeds per setting. The original observed weak matrices, error covariances and refit-pilot joint thresholds were kept fixed. All 84 reference equation instances admitted a fit. Of 213 individual reference-term deletions, 146 were excluded for every refit and 67 admitted explicit coefficient vectors. These counts describe related terms and repeated measurements on fixed benchmark trajectories.

For a fitted vector the audit evaluates both weak inequalities directly. An exclusion uses a nonnegative combination of the restricted homogeneous quadratic forms that is positive definite. Because the target coefficient is fixed to one, that certificate rules out all remaining coefficient values, including arbitrarily large ones. The audit recomputed Gram entries from the original binary64 matrices using exact rational arithmetic. It verified all 729 stored classifications across the primary and supplementary bank comparisons. The resulting proofs apply to the saved constraints. Their physical interpretation also depends on weak discretization and statistical calibration.

Among 186 coordinates with bounded parent enclosures, the old three-proposal check found 21 admissible zero coefficients. Profiling recovered all 21 and another 28. On the 27 coordinates of the unbounded forced-KdV parent regions it found 18 admissible deletions and nine exclusions. In the primary held-out transport check, 122 of 134 fitted deletion witnesses passed both banks on a different noise recording of the same signal without changing coefficients. Each admitted source witness has two destinations. A failed transfer only identifies a particular coefficient vector that failed on that destination.

Allen–Cahn gives a useful control. Deleting the true diffusion term (coefficient 0.0001) was compatible in 9/9 fields. Deleting either the cubic or the linear reaction term was excluded in 9/9 fields for each term. All 18 transferred diffusion-deletion witnesses passed. Other reference supports vary substantially. For example, the measurements excluded all 27 single-term deletions in the KS records and allowed all 12 in iid Duffing. Excluding zero does not determine a coefficient's sign or magnitude.

2. What the weak tests distinguish in forced KdV

On the nine primary forced-KdV fields, deleting dispersion was excluded in every field. A model without the nonlinear term and a model without the known forcing were each compatible in every field. This pattern repeated across the 18 other archived fields under their respective original local/broad constraints, alternate pairs and three-bank intersections. The latter use the 123rd ordered per-draw maximum over three banks on the same 128 bootstrap draws, preserving dependence. Individual-bank comparisons at the common three-bank cutoff showed the same deletion classifications.

Analytic inversion with dispersion as the only predictor found a common admissible coefficient interval of approximately [-0.3179074,-0.1689282] across all 27 original pairs AND the 18 subsequent three-bank intersections. The true dispersion coefficient -1 applied on its own passed the nine iid and nine positively correlated original pairs and failed all nine negative ones. The median least-squares coefficient locked from three iid fields was -0.0002163 and passed none of the 27 original pairs. The compatibility criterion includes measurement error in predictor columns. Ordinary least-squares minimization need not yield a feasible coefficient.

On six new noise fields (seeds 4701/4702; iid and both signs of correlation), each with 128 independent noise refits, the whole-library three-bank test accepted both the full physical equation and a dispersion-only equation fixed in advance at -0.25 in 6/6 cases. It excluded deletion of dispersion in 6/6 after freeing both remaining coefficients. A simultaneous cutoff for just the two locked vectors reduced the threshold to 42–57% of the whole-library threshold, accepted the physical law in 6/6 and rejected the reduced equation in 1/6. Both calibrations covered their measured weak-moment errors in all six. The reduced equation's clean residual norm relative to the target was 0.946 locally and 0.346 on the broad bank, compared with 0.0016 and 0.0006 for the physical law.

3. Directional weak measurements

On six further fields (seeds 4703/4704), a projection learned from an earlier reconstructed field rejected the physical law once and accepted the reduced equation in all six. The matched full-residual norm accepted both in all six. On the iid record where it rejected the physical equation, the projection also missed the measured noise errors for both fixed candidates. Each arm used 32 bootstrap fields to set its noise scale and 128 different fields for the joint cutoff over both equations and all three banks. Thus the paired arms used the same raw fields, nuisance fits, error draws and calibration rule.

As a diagnostic after this test, the absolute cosines between the pilot-predicted contrast and the clean contrast were only 0.066, 0.376 and 0.043 in the local, broad and selected banks. For the known forcing waveform alone the corresponding cosines were 0.738, 0.819 and 0.814. Both the clean oracle direction and the forcing-column direction retained the physical law in 6/6 and rejected the alternative in 3/6 on these same fields. This comparison motivated a separate frozen confirmation of the observable forcing direction.

4. Independent forcing-pattern confirmation

Twelve additional noise realizations (four per profile, seeds 4705–4708) test the observable forcing pattern chosen on the prior six fields. The physical equation passes 12/12 fixed-vector tests. The locked dispersion-only alternative at -0.25 is rejected in 6/12, with substantial variation by noise profile: 2/4 iid, 0/4 positive and 4/4 negative. With the same fields and split calibration, the whole residual norm retains the physical equation in 11/12 and rejects the alternative in 3/12. The old pilot projection retains the physical equation in 11/12 and rejects the alternative in 1/12. The forcing-direction noise bounds cover both measured error vectors on all 12 fields. With four fields per profile these are descriptive counts. The forcing readout discriminates the locked equations in some noise regimes.

Allowing all remaining coefficients to adjust changes the interpretation. Using the local, broad and selected weak banks together with their forcing projections, deletion of dispersion is excluded in 12/12. Dropping the nonlinear term, the forcing term or both together remains feasible in 12/12. Exact rational arithmetic checks every supplied finite or exclusion certificate for this refit. The forcing projection alone even admits deletion of dispersion in 12/12. It provides a complementary direction. The combined four-column simultaneous calibration contains the measured full-support noise errors in 12/12, and admits the complete physical equation at its actual coefficients in 12/12. This calibration covers all surviving coefficient values on the specified support and differs from the narrower test of just two fixed vectors.

There is a single reduced equation that passes every combined restriction across the 12 fresh fields. Exact scalar inversion yields a common dispersion-only interval of approximately [-0.2179213,-0.1767705]. The rational choice -1/5 satisfies all 72 original inequalities (six readouts per record) strictly, without per-record coefficient adjustment. This interval and witness were computed after seeing the fields to diagnose their ambiguity. They are neither an estimate of the physical dispersion coefficient nor a joint confidence interval calibrated across 12 records. Together with the clean residual gap, this identifies a specific misleading alternative for testing independent measurements on different observed trajectories.

Scope and reproducibility

The studies use saved raw systems, corrected weak integrals, nuisance noise refits and finite-dimensional algebra. No candidate dynamics are integrated. Every fresh field has independent observation noise on the same saved forced trajectory. The benchmark specifies the forcing waveform. Explicit constants are kept whenever present in the support. The separate ode_simple example was outside the core-15 archive used here. There are 24 fresh observations and 3,648 bootstrap noise refits. All fresh observations and bootstrap fields were regenerated for hash checks. A first projection-run launch passed the profile and seed in reverse order and stopped during initialization. The corrected launch used the frozen seeds and unchanged protocol.

Primary per-term outputs are in core_deletions.csv, certificates.json and heldout_transfer.csv. The fixed-equation comparison is in forcing_audit.json and forcing_confirmation.pdf. The unrestricted deletion test is in forcing_profile.json and shared_dispersion.json. Method protocols, decision code, coefficient certificates, replay checks and source hashes accompany the results.
