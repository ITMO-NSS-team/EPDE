All-system test: joint noise correction and coefficient-sensitive validation
2026-11-16

All 45 runs are complete: 15 systems x three fresh noise seeds. Both proposed
fixes and their combination were tested. Neither is a universal replacement.
Correction reduces a conditional errors-in-variables bias but increases
variance and can leave an indefinite normal matrix. Mixed transfer validation
fixes the large KS selection failure on these records, but substantially
worsens wave and forced KdV. The full results support separate treatment of
insufficient coefficient sensitivity and excessive validation noise.

Scope and frozen design

Systems: ac, burgers, burgers_inviscid, duffing, kdv, kdv_cossin, ks, lorenz,
lv, ns, ode, pde_compound, pde_divide, vdp, wave. Noise: independent additive
Gaussian at 5%, fitting/inner seeds 12/13/14, outer records 15/16/17. These
are new observation-noise realizations on the existing benchmark fields,
not new trajectories. Three interleaved training rotations per record.

The catalog has 20 reference equation slots, giving 60 reference/seed cases;
including registered equivalents gives 93 equation/seed cases. This is a
known-support coefficient and fit-selection experiment, NOT 60 successful
discoveries, a structural survival count, or a new evolutionary search.

Each native local/broad/mixed bank is fitted by OLS, residual-covariance IRLS,
pooled-covariance GLS, corrected OLS and corrected GLS. Both corrections
subtract centered predictor/predictor AND predictor/target noise moments.
Corrected GLS and GLS have identical training-only weights. The correction
requires a positive full-rank normalized predictor Gram at 1e-12; indefinite
cases are recorded without a signed inverse, clipping, ridge or fallback.

Three validation rules compare the identical six options: local/broad/mixed
x IRLS/corrected GLS. Baseline broad_white is the existing pooled-whitened
native broad score. broad_transfer maps a broad weak residual through the
training pilot's GLS coefficient left inverse, then measures the implied
coefficient correction on a richer training spectral bank. mixed_transfer
does the same with stacked local/broad moments and is the primary new rule.
There are no tuned bank penalties or clean-data choices. Fitting and all
score transforms, scales, coefficients and choices are serialized and hashed
before generating each outer record. No refit follows selection.

Transfer scores are SUPPORT-SPECIFIC. They compare methods for the same
specified equation, project out residual directions outside its predictor
span, and are not general-purpose structural-discovery objectives. Their
training spectral geometry is frozen from old seed 0; the field values used
to construct the transform are reconstructed from the training group only.

Compared with broad selection, mixed transfer improves every KS record and
worsens every forced-KdV record. Its KS median improves about 935-fold;
wave's median worsens about 6.1-fold and forced KdV's about 13.6-fold. Adding
local information matters: broad_transfer alone still has severe KS errors.
No single selector should replace the current research controls universally.

What the independent outer records and clean controls show

Selection is evaluated on independent observation noise after its choices
are fixed. No outer score is used to rescue a failed choice or select a
different scoring rule. Negative signed cross scores are retained.

For KS, mixed transfer chooses the most accurate available fit in 4/9
reference folds, versus 2/9 for broad_white. More significantly, its median
ratio to the best available fit is 1.135, versus 2.402 for broad_white and
4566.6 for broad_transfer. Using clean fields with the same frozen score
transforms, mixed transfer identifies the best option in 8/9 folds, versus
5/9 for broad_white. This supports improved sensitivity when local moments
are included; simply amplifying broad moments is not sufficient.

For wave, ALL three scores choose the most accurate option in 9/9 CLEAN
comparisons. On noisy inner records, mixed transfer does so only 2/9 times,
and the broad scores 1/9. Independent outer and inner mixed-transfer choices
agree only 2/9 times. This separates the failure from a clean metric ranking
problem: the noisy validation moment errors overwhelm useful distinctions.
Example: wave seed 12, fold 0 selects corrected mixed coefficient 0.092654
instead of exact 0.04. Its clean energy is 1.73253, while broad IRLS has
2.26584e-6 and is correctly preferred by clean transfer validation.

For forced KdV, broad_white chooses the most accurate option in 7/9 folds,
mixed transfer in 4/9. The clean mixed-transfer score succeeds in 7/9; it
therefore has both noisy-ranking failures and remaining deterministic
distortion on these fits. In seed 13 fold 1, mixed transfer selects broad
corrected coefficients (-26.4034,-6.6338,7.6866) with energy 3.68499, although
mixed corrected GLS has energy 0.079945. The independent outer score also
prefers that bad broad correction. Fresh noise alone does not ensure that
an individual conditional score measures the desired equation accuracy.

Detailed per-fold inner/outer/clean disagreement and large errors are in
selection_diagnostics.csv and large_selection_errors.json. The latter's
10-times reporting threshold is post-hoc listing only, never a fitting or
selection threshold. Counts over folds are dependent descriptive counts.

What noise correction fixes, and what it costs

The matched mixed GLS comparison isolates correction from changing weights:
33 improvements versus 26 regressions, one incomplete case. Forced KdV's
mixed median improves from GLS 0.099353 to corrected GLS 0.054871. Its
conditional bias is real and partly removable, but this does not make every
corrected fit reliable. Corrected Gram eigenvalues can approach zero or turn
negative, and inverse fitting can then have large variance or fail.

The separate conditional experiment reuses frozen forced-KdV seeds 6/7/8,
with 64 independent pilot-noise refits per bank/fold. It does not count as
another fresh-seed replication and does not choose any core15 method.
All refits use fixed pooled weights. Bias is measured relative to noiseless
OLS/GLS with the SAME weights, since no noise correction should be subtracted
from truly noiseless data. Coordinate order is (u*u_x, u_xxx, cos(t)*sin(x)).

For mixed GLS, coordinate-wise median bootstrap drift changes from
  (0.83430, 0.12084, -0.13986)
to corrected mixed GLS
  (0.06949, -0.01965, 0.02295).
But median bootstrap SD rises from
  (1.21179, 0.03393, 0.03821)
to
  (2.11011, 0.08079, 0.09110).
Both mixed methods succeed in all 576 refits. This is a clear conditional
bias/variance tradeoff, not a free improvement. Broad corrected GLS succeeds
575/576 times, local corrected GLS 526/576. Corrected OLS is less feasible:
local 500/576, broad 482/576, mixed 509/576. Successful-only bootstrap
summaries are explicitly conditional on convergence/feasibility.

All conditional refits total 6,912: OLS and GLS each 1,728/1,728 successful;
corrected GLS 1,677/1,728; corrected OLS 1,491/1,728. These hold the estimated
noise model, training reconstruction and covariance weights fixed and do not
calibrate uncertainty in those estimated quantities. The original pilot can
still represent inaccurate coefficients, as diagnosed in the previous study.

Feasibility, constants and implementation checks

The main 45-run experiment contains 4,185 fits, including equivalents:
  OLS:            837/837 successful.
  GLS:            837/837 successful.
  IRLS:           811/837 successful; 24 singular residual covariances and
                  two nonconvergences.
  Corrected OLS:  748/837 successful; 89 indefinite predictor Grams.
  Corrected GLS:  760/837 successful; 77 indefinite predictor Grams.
All 837 support/rotation/score combinations are feasible, and all 837
selection decisions have at least two feasible fit options. No forced
selection occurs. The selected methods can still be inaccurate despite
passing numerical rank and positive-definiteness tests.

Most correction infeasibility occurs among wave equivalents: 74/77 corrected
GLS failures are on wave; two are inviscid Burgers equivalents and one forced
KdV. All 24 singular IRLS covariances occur in local/mixed KdV fits. The two
IRLS nonconvergences are local/mixed forced KdV. Failed fits remain in the
tables and are never averaged away or replaced with tuned regularization.

The initial three Duffing attempts stopped on a geometric bounds assertion.
Its time coordinates are stored with float32 rounding; extrapolating the
first native grid spacing places the final group-2 support endpoint 1.71e-6
beyond the last observed time. The coordinate-only adapter uses actual
historical window centers and the largest common symmetric radius inside
their intended native spans. It changes no integer window plan or statistical
choice, performs no extrapolation and relaxes no tolerance. All three
repaired Duffing runs completed. Original failed logs and process status
are retained; completion_audit.json verifies 45 final results, no missing
cases, and records the separate adapter source hash.

Population-moment tests verify exact removal of joint predictor/target
noise, and detect intentionally omitting the cross term. Feature-unit
invariance, planted coefficient-effect recovery by transfer validation,
indefinite/rank rejection, selection isolation and finite scores all pass.
ODE_simple's two phase identities and energy identity retain constants
1, 1.3 and 2.69 in separate zero-noise controls; phase identities use no
derivative powers, and energy uses a squared first derivative. The catalog's
ODE benchmark is distinct from ODE_simple. No valid identity is removed.

Audit passes for all new noise records, source/input/result hashes,
registered supports, 10,845 validation-window support checks, stored score
transforms, frozen selection replay, unchanged coefficients after outer
access, and complete-case aggregation. Full weight-derived score transforms
and scales are saved in frozen/ and results/. The first-stage process log
says 42/45 because it includes the three original Duffing stops; the final
completion audit includes their successful repairs and confirms 45/45.

Decision

Keep the new correction and transfer scores as experimental ablations.
Do not globally replace the estimator or objective based on the average
improvement count. KS benefits from added local information; wave needs a
lower-variance validation estimate; forced KdV needs reliable coefficient
fitting and uncertainty that covers both pilot/model error and correction
variance. Additional independent measurements or justified moment averaging
could test the wave variance issue. A statistically identified response to
weak/indefinite corrected information is still needed for fitting. Those are
research directions, not tested fixes in this experiment.
