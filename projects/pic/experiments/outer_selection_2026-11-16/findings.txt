Outer measurement validation and coefficient sensitivity, 2026-11-16

Automatic local/broad selection fails the new-seed test. It improves forced
KdV relative to fixed mixed fitting, but worsens AC, wave and KS in all
three records each. The selected KS fit has median frozen clean residual
energy 0.510217 versus 4.71233e-5 for fixed mixed fitting. The independent
outer score can barely distinguish seriously inaccurate KS fits, and a
clean broad score also misranks one observed comparison. A new noise split
alone cannot repair the score's inadequate sensitivity to the desired
equation accuracy. Do not promote this selector.

Forced KdV has a separate fitting problem: conditional uncertainty is much
smaller than the actual dispersion/forcing coefficient errors, and the
pilot already has inaccurate coefficients. Conditional bootstrap drift is
consistent with an errors-in-variables bias left by covariance-reweighted
least squares. This motivates a controlled correction of the fitting normal
equations, rather than more tuning of the broad validation selector.

Study design

Twelve runs on AC, forced KdV, wave and KS. Training and inner selection use
fresh 5% Gaussian noise seeds 6/7/8; paired independent outer measurement
records use 9/10/11. These are independent noise realizations on the SAME
four clean benchmark fields, not independent trajectories. Three rotated
interleaved groups per record: one fits, the other two supply signed inner
cross residuals. Outer evaluation uses the corresponding two groups from
the independent noise record. Folds overlap and are not independent samples.

Known equation supports only: 12 reference/seed cases, 24 registered-
equation/seed cases including four wave equivalents. No 15-system rerun,
structural discovery count or candidate equation solve is claimed.

Geometries are unchanged from native_moments seed 3, which froze them from
historical geometry and coordinates. Native local, broad and stacked mixed
fits use corrected OLS and the unchanged 512-draw residual-covariance IRLS.
The two selection rules choose ONLY between local and broad IRLS:
  Primary: native broad whitened signed inner cross residual.
  Comparator: prior interpolated broad whitened signed inner cross residual.
Mixed and OLS fits are fixed controls, not extra choices. Ties choose local;
if only one fit converges, selection is explicitly forced. Weights are
training-only and common to all fitted equations. No refit after selection.

For each run, coefficients, weight diagnostics and inner decisions were saved
and hashed BEFORE generating its outer record. Clean spectral scoring and exact
coefficient errors were calculated only after these decisions. The frozen
JSON files contain no outer or clean evaluation fields. The completion audit
replays every choice and verifies that outer evaluation changes no fit.
Serialization detail relative to the protocol: full weight matrices remain
fixed in memory; the frozen JSON stores their covariance/rank diagnostics,
not the matrices themselves. They are never re-estimated on outer data.
Raw operator corrections use each measurement group's own estimated noise,
while fitting/selection never use the outer record's noise estimates.

Independent outer evaluation and why it does not rescue selection

The primary selector chooses the more accurate available fit by frozen
clean energy in 25/36 reference folds, including two forced choices. The
comparator succeeds in 26/36. These fold counts are descriptive, not 36
independent tests. The independent outer score agrees with the chosen bank
in 24/36 and 25/36 respectively. All signed cross scores, including negative
ones, are retained. With three outer records per system this is a limited
reliability test, not a precise estimate of selector performance.

The KS failure is concrete. On seed 7, training fold 0:
  Local: coefficients (-0.99970, -0.99742, -0.99764), energy 3.13975e-5.
  Broad: coefficients (-0.99776, -0.98029, -0.58529), energy 3.48943.
  Exact coefficients: (-1, -1, -1).
The independent native outer scores are -6.27539e-6 and -6.25653e-6. Their
difference is only 1.89e-8 despite a greater than 100,000-fold difference in
the frozen clean residual energy. These scores use a different metric and
normalization, so their absolute scale cannot be compared directly with
spectral residual energy; their near equality is the relevant observation.

There is also deterministic metric mismatch. For KS seed 6, fold 2, the
clean native broad score prefers the broad fit (8.13439e-7) to local
(1.08638e-5), while frozen clean energy prefers local (3.14954e-5) over
broad (1.96996e-2). Thus noise reduction alone does not make the current
broad metric agree with the accuracy target. Broad-only weighting is too
insensitive to some coefficient directions in these data.

Wave shows large common fluctuations in absolute cross scores: the primary
selector's record scores are inner (-3.17288, 1.83855, -1.29801) versus
outer (6.81147, 3.88486, -5.48959), while its clean native validation scores
are (0.007442, 0.057126, 0.013169). Shared fluctuations can cancel in paired
fit comparisons, so absolute score variation alone does not establish how
uncertain a particular ranking is. The wrong choices and outer comparisons
are reported directly in decisions.csv and per_case.csv.

Forced-KdV coefficient sensitivity

Coefficient order is u*u_x, u_xxx, cos(t)*sin(x); exact values (-6,-1,1).
Coordinate-wise medians over the nine broad reference fits:
  Observed broad IRLS:       (-0.492258, -0.450943, 0.373867)
  Noiseless pilot IRLS:      (-1.046829, -0.493602, 0.432136)
  Clean native OLS control:  (-6.016944, -1.000271, 1.000190)
These medians are diagnostics, not an averaged fitted equation.

Linearized conditional standard deviations for broad IRLS have coordinate
medians (2.84245, 0.055745, 0.067350). Median signed actual errors in units
of each fit's own conditional SD are (1.856, 9.849, -9.297). Dispersion and
forcing errors exceed three such SDs in every broad reference fold. These
conditional SDs hold the pilot, noise model and weights fixed; they are
not calibrated unconditional confidence intervals. They demonstrate that
the current conditional uncertainty model does not account for the full
error, rather than proving a formal significance level.

Normalized weighted-design eigenvalue ratios have medians 0.2136 (local,
seven converged fits), 0.00765 (broad) and 0.01319 (mixed). The broad weak
direction mixes the nonlinear, dispersion and forcing coefficients. Near-
singularity alone is not a sufficient explanation: local fits can have
an apparently adequate normalized design and still be seriously biased.

64 independent pilot-noise refits per bank/fold, using the separate frozen
512-draw covariance model, give broad bootstrap SD medians
(2.40856, 0.060549, 0.073073). These are still small relative to the actual
dispersion/forcing errors. Broad bootstrap mean displacement from noiseless
pilot IRLS has median (1.08164, 0.149484, -0.178196). The pilot's relation is
already wrong; conditional resampling around it cannot establish that the
original equation has been recovered. This diagnostic does not by itself
prove that pilot reconstruction causes the observed raw-fit error, because
the raw fit uses the pilot to estimate covariance rather than as its target.

Post-hoc normal-equation diagnosis (not a new estimator test)

The existing IRLS step holds its current residual covariance fixed and
solves weighted least squares. It does not subtract predictor/target noise
cross moments from the normal equations. At the noiseless pilot solution
c0, centered joint operator draws estimate the expected normal-score term
E[delta_X^T W (delta_y - delta_X*c0)], with W the fixed inverse residual
covariance. This term need not be zero even when mean operator corrections
are accurate. Covariance weighting alone therefore leaves an EIV bias.

Using the pilot signal Gram plus predictor-noise Gram, the predicted
fixed-weight coefficient displacement has broad median
(0.84647, 0.136442, -0.162841), compared with the actual iterated bootstrap
displacement (1.08164, 0.149484, -0.178196). They agree in direction and
rough scale, not exactly: the latter also updates the weights. The largest
generalized predictor-noise/signal Gram ratio has median 0.493 for broad,
1.564 for local and 0.695 for mixed. Thus predictor noise is not negligible
in the most affected direction. This analysis was added after outcomes and
changes no fit or selector; it is a causal hypothesis supported by a
conditional model, not a validated production fix.

Convergence and audits

Main study: 213/216 IRLS fits converge and all 216 OLS fits succeed. The
three IRLS failures are forced KdV seeds 6 and 8, training fold 1, local;
and wave seed 7, training fold 0, local equivalent_3. Both selectors mark
the corresponding broad choices as forced. No failed fit is averaged away.

Conditional bootstrap: 3,456 total refits. All 1,728 OLS fits succeed.
IRLS succeeds in 435/576 local, 575/576 broad and 551/576 mixed refits
(1,561/1,728 overall). Summaries of successful bootstrap coefficients are
explicitly conditional on convergence; especially local results can be
affected by the 141 failures. Full status counts and samples are retained.

Checks pass for selection isolation, deterministic ties/failure behavior,
signed-score algebra, linearized covariance consistency and feature-unit
invariance. The main audit verifies fresh noise replay; disjoint seed/group
sets; fixed source/input hashes; unchanged geometries/registered supports;
frozen selection replay; unchanged coefficients after outer access; feasible
validation weights; finite scores; and all bootstrap outcomes. Constant
columns are checked during every run. The prior three ODE_simple identity
controls are inherited with a recorded hash; no new ODE_simple search is
claimed. Report-level aggregation and artifact hashes are recorded separately.

Next experimental decision

Keep fixed mixed fitting as a research control and reject automatic broad-
score selection for now. A useful next fitting ablation is a noise-corrected
joint normal-equation step on the same frozen training moments, checked first
against conditional bootstrap bias and then on fresh records. Such correction
can yield indefinite or weak information matrices; retain those failures
explicitly rather than insert a tuned ridge. This is related to the earlier
CLSM work, but needs a matched test with the current native moments and joint
predictor/target covariance, not an assumption that it will solve the problem.
Separately, validation needs sensitivity to the coefficient directions that
broad moments miss. Neither proposed change is implemented or validated here.
