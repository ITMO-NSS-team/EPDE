Constants are necessary in ODE_simple's invariants; raw weak validation is
too noisy to replace the existing score in the targeted failures.
2026-11-16

The user's ODE_simple_discovery is u=sin(t)+1.3*cos(t), sampled at 200 points
over [0,4*pi], with first derivatives allowed. It is NOT the forced-oscillator
'ode' in the earlier core-15 registry. Those earlier counts did not include
this simple example. See ode_simple_scope.txt for the source/alias correction.

Three valid identities are:
  u' cos(t) + u sin(t) = 1
  u cos(t) - u' sin(t) = 1.3
  (u')^2 + u^2 = 2.69.
The first two require no power of the derivative. The third is retained as
a valid identity as requested, but needs a squared first derivative if it
is to be a search candidate. None requires u'' in the search. For a general
u=A*sin(t)+B*cos(t), these constants are A, B and A^2+B^2: integration
constants determined by the trajectory. They are not additive forcing in
the universal oscillator equation. Their low discrepancy and low dyad are
correct behavior and must not be counted as failures of the objective.

The three identities agree with analytical values to maximum errors
3.33e-16, 2.22e-16 and 8.88e-16, respectively. Using the source's FD4 and
10-point edge exclusion, ordinary least squares gives:
  u' cos(t) = -0.9999994702 u sin(t) + 0.9999994702
  u' sin(t) =  0.9999994702 u cos(t) - 1.2999993113
  (u')^2   = -0.9999989404 u^2       + 2.6899971498.
The tiny coefficient shifts are the finite-difference response to sinusoids.
Their normalized residual energies are about 3.30e-29, 3.38e-29 and 1.34e-28.
Refitting after deleting the constants raises these to 0.9771, 0.9861 and
0.8963. Thus the constants do substantive work even without observation noise.

Independent-group continuous weak fits confirm the same conclusion. At 5%
added noise, median clean residual energies over seeds 0/1/2 are:
  Identity       With constant       Without constant
  Phase sin      1.9397e-4           0.99521
  Phase cos      1.6707e-4           0.94887
  Energy         8.0782e-4           0.81049
The energy offsets across seeds are 2.6112, 2.6925 and 2.6477. Phase-sin
offsets are 1.0071, 0.9947 and 1.0005; phase-cos offsets are -1.2860,
-1.3066 and -1.2988 in the fitted target orientation above. This is a
controlled known-Gaussian-noise, fixed-panel test, not new evolutionary
discovery. All coefficients are trained on one raw group and evaluated on
the other two. The source function itself currently ignores its noise_level
argument; this experiment explicitly adds the 5% perturbations.

The constant's dyad contribution is essentially zero for the noiseless valid
identities: 0, 6.66e-16 and 4.27e-16 for energy/phase-sin/phase-cos. Stable
nonzero constants are not automatically penalized. A different behavior
appears for a redundant intercept: u'=cos(t)-1.3*sin(t) already needs no
offset. Adding one produces an FD coefficient 1.18e-16 with no meaningful
residual improvement, yet the weak dyad's constant contribution is 0.7333.
Its MAD/abs(median) normalization is ill-conditioned near zero; an order-one
relative score need not imply a large absolute error. At 5% noise, adding
this redundant intercept increases median clean energy from 4.6625e-5 to
5.9076e-5. A cubic autonomous u'=f(u)+constant remains inaccurate (clean
energy about 0.96-0.99), so an offset cannot replace the missing state/phase.

The separate audit of the existing core-15 panel covers all 45 system runs,
60 equation/seed cases. None of that catalog's registered references or
equivalents contains an additive constant; this says nothing against the
ODE_simple identities outside the catalog. The experimental fitting code
has no hidden intercept: constants are explicit columns, and dyad uses
fit_intercept=False. Every original dyad value reproduces exactly.

Among the six existing failed cases, constant-containing models dominate
in only two (forced KdV seeds 0 and 1). All six also have a dominating model
without a constant. Deleting constant candidates would therefore not remove
any of these six reference failures. Reference-plus-constant dominates its
reference in just one case (forced KdV seed 0). Adding a jointly fitted
constant improves clean reference-fit energy in 10/60 cases and worsens it
in the other 50; fitting an offset can sometimes compensate coefficient bias,
but this does not establish a physical forcing term.

As a diagnostic, omitting only the explicit constant's dyad contribution
while leaving fitted coefficients/discrepancy fixed reduces retained
registered structures from 54/60 to 53/60, losing Burgers seed 2. This models
one effect of an unscored intercept, not a fully rerun hidden-intercept search.
It gives no support for making offsets free in the instability objective.
Per-candidate and reference-plus-constant comparisons are saved in
constant_candidates.csv, constant_pairs.csv and constant_cases.csv.

Raw weak validation experiment:
Existing coefficients were frozen. Raw groups were mapped to the original
fine grid with fixed natural-cubic interpolation, without time smoothing or
spatial filtering. The weak quadrature was unchanged on retained rows.
Gaussian pair contractions correct degree-2/3 monomials analytically, using
propagated within-field covariance. Independent held-out residual cross
products remove the within-group squared-noise floor. This correction is
exact only for the assumed known Gaussian covariance; the main experiment
uses the earlier estimated per-group covariance.

Completed wave, KS and forced KdV x seeds 0/1/2: nine system/equation cases.
AC was checked for feasibility: each seed has at least one training fold
whose full-span weak windows leave no common interior support for the other
raw groups. We do not extrapolate or silently substitute a new window. These
three AC cases are explicitly unscored. Initial wave runs before the support
guard adjustment are archived and excluded from the nine final cases.
The primary wave/KS test failed to establish reliable improvement, so this
raw-validation method was not expanded to all 15 systems.

KS and forced-KdV edge rows that would require extrapolation were excluded
from raw AND smooth/clean controls. The original full-bank normalization is
fixed. Unmasked smooth scores reproduce the stored baseline to maximum
scaled error 5.86e-11. Original and matched-interior smooth retention are
both 5/9 for baseline coefficients; raw validation also retains 5/9.
For the previously tested joint-risk coefficients the counts are 5/9 smooth
and 6/9 raw, but this secondary count is not sufficient evidence of a fix.
All frozen fits and all registered equivalents remain in the panel.

Across six fixed reference-fitting options, raw validation selects the most
accurate local KS option in all three seeds, but ranks the best clean option
incorrectly in all three wave seeds. Selecting from these validation scores
would require another evaluation split; these rankings are diagnostics only.
Wave seed 0: the clean-best broad OLS fit scores 0.0423 WORSE than baseline
under raw validation, with paired conditional SD 0.3796. Noise prevents this
raw score from reliably resolving the relevant difference.

Exact-coefficient controls show the scale of this problem. Wave seed 0 raw
score is 3.7019 with conditional SD 4.1402, whereas the clean control is
1.4329e-4. Wave seed 1 SD is 22.74 and seed 2 SD is 14.36. Forced-KdV seed 2
has exact-coefficient raw score -27625.8 and SD 60414.3, versus clean energy
6.80e-7. Negative cross scores are kept signed, as specified. Removing the
noise-floor bias does not remove variance or guarantee useful finite-sample
rankings. KS exact scores are much quieter, about 2.45e-5 to 3.16e-5 with SD
about 4.92e-5 to 5.86e-5, consistent with retaining the good local fits.

Clean subsample controls isolate deterministic interpolation/finite-difference
error. Wave seed 0 clean exact score changes only 1.4329066e-4 -> 1.4328532e-4.
KS's near-zero full-grid exact score rises to about 5.24e-7 on interpolated
subsamples; this error is reported, not treated as noise. These controls do
not explain the enormous raw wave/forced-KdV dispersion.

Uncertainty uses 128 corrected operator draws and 512 shared empirical
perturbations, preserving paired-candidate and shared-group dependence.
The noisy-data reconstruction is used only as a variance proxy, never in
the raw point score. Fits and covariance estimates are held fixed; reported
SDs are conditional diagnostics, not calibrated confidence intervals. The
64/128 comparison is saved in raw_controls.csv, not independent replication.

Verification: Gaussian polynomial expectations by exact Gauss-Hermite
quadrature; covariance propagation checked against an independent dense
small-grid calculation; constants integrate to one; a constant's derivative
vanishes; independent cross-products have no squared-noise floor; original
dyad/smooth scores reproduce; source/input hashes and finite outputs pass.
No candidate ODE/PDE integration, PINNs, production edits or commits.
All experiment processes have finished.

Implication: keep constants in the search and score them explicitly. Credit
the valid ODE_simple invariants under the actual derivative/token restriction.
The six core failures require more than an intercept rule. Raw validation on
the present compact supports is too variable; a future test should reduce
moment variance while preserving identifiable derivative information, with
quadrature and noise estimates checked together. No new weight, threshold,
automatic intercept deletion or production objective has been introduced.

Background only: Messenger and Bortz's WSINDy consistency analysis documents
noise-induced spurious polynomial terms: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16000 .
Our identities and finite-sample outcomes above are derived/tested locally;
the paper does not validate this proposed raw-moment score.
