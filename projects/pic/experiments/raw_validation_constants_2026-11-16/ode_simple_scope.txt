Dataset and search-scope correction from the user's clarification

The requested example is ODE_simple_discovery in projects/pic/data/ode/ode.py:
  t = linspace(0,4*pi,200), u = sin(t)+1.3*cos(t), max_deriv_order=1.
It is distinct from epde_bench.datasets.load('ode'), the forced oscillator
used by the earlier core-15 experiment. The robot loader also uses the alias
'ode' for this simple analytic example; the alias alone is ambiguous.

The user confirmed that the first-derivative phase identities are relevant,
and then asked to retain the energy identity as another correct identity.
We therefore keep all three controls without conflating search availability:
  u' cos(t) + u sin(t) = 1
  u cos(t) - u' sin(t) = 1.3
  (u')^2 + u^2 = 2.69

The first two use only the first power of the first derivative. The third
uses a squared FIRST derivative, not a second derivative. If derivative powers
are disabled it is a mathematical control outside that restricted library.
No u'' candidate was tested. The energy and phase targets in this diagnostic
are decorated derivatives. A separate plain-target-only policy could forbid
those target forms; this study does not override production target policies.

For the general oscillator trajectory u=A*sin(t)+B*cos(t), the right-hand
constants are A, B and A^2+B^2. Here A=1 and B=1.3. They encode initial
conditions/invariants, not additive external forcing in u''+u=0.
We keep them as valid trajectory identities rather than false discoveries.
This classification is distinct from claiming a complete, generally valid
evolution equation was recovered by a full search.

The source function itself does not apply its noise_level argument to x.
Its noiseless FD4/10-point-boundary setup is checked directly. The additional
5% experiments explicitly add noise with seeds 0/1/2 and use known iid
Gaussian covariance as a controlled diagnostic. They are not a reproduction
of an evolutionary search or unknown-noise estimation.
